The objective of backgammon is to move all 15 of your checkers around the board into your home board and then remove them before your opponent does.

Setup and Board
The board has 24 long triangles called points (or pips), divided into four quadrants.
Each player gets 15 checkers of one color.
Checkers start in a fixed layout: 2 on the 24-point, 5 on the 13-point, 3 on the 8-point, and 5 on the 6-point.
Players move their checkers in opposing, horseshoe-shaped directions toward their own home board.

Movement and Dice
Both players roll one die to start; the higher number goes first and uses both dice numbers for that first turn.
After the start, players roll two dice and take turns moving checkers forward by the numbers shown.
Numbers on two dice are separate moves; you can move one checker twice or two different checkers once.
Rolling doubles lets you play that number four separate times.
You can only land on an open point—a point with no opposing checkers, your own checkers, or only a single opposing checker (called a blot). 
Two or more opposing checkers block the point.

Hitting and the Bar
Landing on a single opposing checker (a blot) "hits" it, sending it to the middle dividing strip called the bar.
If you have a checker on the bar, your priority is re-entering it into the opponent's home board before making any other moves.

Bearing Off and Winning
When all 15 of your checkers are inside your home board, you can start bearing off (removing them from the board using matching dice rolls).
The first player to bear off all 15 checkers wins.
Wins can be worth more points: a single win, a gammon (if the loser removed no checkers), or a backgammon (if the loser removed no checkers and still has a piece on the bar or winner's home board).
